Background
I am originally from Myeik, a coastal town in southern Myanmar. Growing up in a rapidly changing environment, I became deeply curious about how technology shapes the world around us. That curiosity gradually evolved into ambition—to pursue something beyond the limitations I saw back home. At the time, that meant getting an education abroad, and I eventually chose to study in the United States. My first step was learning English, which opened the door not only to communication but to entirely new ways of thinking. Through my high school studies, I was exposed to subjects like math and science in a new light. A formative moment came when I began using Python and the Pygame library to visualize geometric problems—drawing shapes, experimenting with movement, and watching code turn into something visual and interactive. That hands-on experience was the spark that solidified my passion for computing and guided me toward the field of computer science.
Education
After completing my studies in Yangon, I set my sights on deepening my understanding of technology in a more structured and rigorous environment. That pursuit led me to Ohlone College in California, where I began laying the groundwork for my education in computer science. During my time there, I developed core competencies in programming—particularly in Python and C++—while also learning to think critically through courses in algorithms, data structures, and discrete mathematics. It was at Ohlone that I first began to appreciate how elegantly code could be used to solve complex, real-world problems. Beyond the classroom, I spent a lot of time building and debugging small projects, breaking things down to see how they worked, and gradually sharpening my problem-solving skills through hands-on experimentation. This practical, iterative approach became central to how I learn and grow as a developer. Now, as I continue my academic path at California State University, Fullerton (CSUF), I find myself gravitating toward more specialized areas within computer science. I’m particularly drawn to artificial intelligence, computer vision, and systems-level development—fields that merge technical complexity with tangible, impactful outcomes. Whether it’s experimenting with OpenCV to process visual data or trying to understand the nuances of efficient system design, I’m constantly exploring where my interests align with my strengths. While I haven’t fully settled on a specialization yet, I see that uncertainty not as a limitation, but as space to grow. Each class, project, and personal exploration helps clarify what excites me most and what kind of problems I want to spend my career solving.
Skills
I’ve developed a well-rounded foundation in both front-end and back-end programming through hands-on experience and coursework. My core programming languages include Python, JavaScript, C++, and SQL, which I’ve applied in projects ranging from geometric visualizations with Pygame to server logic and database operations. In web development, I’m proficient in HTML and CSS, and have built interactive interfaces using JavaScript. On the backend, I’ve worked with Express.js to create login systems and route handlers, and experimented with connecting user input and data services.
In managing data, I’m comfortable using tools like Google Sheets and Excel for organizing, tracking, and analyzing information — but I often found them limiting or inefficient for the kinds of tasks I needed. This led me to develop personal tools and custom web apps that offered more control, automation, and structure, such as dynamic interfaces for record-keeping and reporting. I’ve also explored OCR development using the Tesseract engine in Python, applying it to extract and preprocess text from image data. Additionally, I have experience working with JSON files and LaTeX for structured document generation. Version control with Git and collaboration via GitHub are part of my regular workflow, and I continue to refine my skills in modular code design, debugging, and building client-server systems.
Interests
I like building stuff that actually works and solves small annoyances — especially when existing tools feel too bloated or not flexible enough, like Google Sheets or Excel. That’s what got me into full-stack development in the first place: making something lightweight but useful, with control over both the backend and the front. I’ve been messing around with OCR using Tesseract because I think it’s cool to get machines to read things the way we do — and I’m interested in the potential it has. I’m also trying to figure out how to build apps across platforms — iOS, macOS, Android — mostly because I use all of them and want my tools to work everywhere. I’m not totally sure what I want to specialize in yet, but I really like the feeling of connecting ideas into systems that do something real.
